Pet Flight Companion (Flight Nanny) Near Charlotte, NC

Your pet flies in the cabin, with someone who cares.

A flight companion, sometimes called a flight nanny, flies with your small dog or cat in the passenger cabin, from your door to the person waiting at the other end. No cargo hold, and no flying alone.

What you’ll need to have ready

We’ll send a checklist with your quote. The common items:

  • A pet that fits the cabin rulesMost airlines require the pet and carrier to weigh 20 lb or less and fit under the seat. Check your airline.
  • An airline-approved carrierWith enough room and ventilation for your pet to turn around and lie down.
  • A health certificateFor interstate travel, a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection from a licensed vet, usually within 10 days of travel.
  • Current vaccinationsRecords showing rabies and other required vaccines. A microchip is recommended.
  • Medications and a familiar blanketPlus any food and a small bag of treats.
  • A named person at the destinationWith a phone number, ready to meet your pet at the gate or curb.

How the price works

Every trip is different, so each one is quoted.

  • A written quote up frontCovering pickup, the flight, layovers and drop-off, so there are no surprises.
  • The airline’s pet fee is separateYou pay the airline directly. Your companion’s own ticket is also separate and shown in the quote.
  • What changes the priceDistance, layovers, the number of pets, and any medical needs on the way.

Questions

About flight companion

Which pets can fly with a companion?

Small dogs and cats that fit in an airline-approved carrier under the seat. Larger pets can’t fly in the cabin, so ground transport is usually the better choice.

Why is there no set price?

Distance, dates, layovers and airline fees change every trip. We give you a written quote with each part listed, so you can compare it with any other.

What if my flight is delayed or canceled?

We’ll agree in writing, before you book, what happens and what it costs if plans change. Your pet is never left alone while we sort it out.

Do you need the health certificate?

For interstate travel, airlines and states generally require a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection from a licensed vet. We’ll tell you what applies to your route.

Can you fly my pet internationally?

Not at this time. International travel has more rules and paperwork than we can promise to handle well.
