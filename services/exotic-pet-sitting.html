Before the first visit

We go over everything at the free meet-and-greet and look at the setup in person.

  • A written care sheetWhat each pet eats and when, how much, heat and light settings, and anything that’s normal for them.
  • Food and supplies laid outEnough for the whole trip, plus extra. Frozen food labeled and stored where we can reach it.
  • Habitat settings and what to watch forThe right temperature and humidity ranges, and what to do if a lamp, heater or filter fails.
  • Species, age and handlingWhat kind of animal each one is, how old, and whether they may be handled at all.
  • An exotic vet and a backup contactNot every vet sees birds and reptiles, so keep yours handy, along with an emergency contact.

Questions

About birds, reptiles, fish and small pets

Which animals do you care for?

Birds, reptiles, aquarium fish, and small mammals such as rabbits and guinea pigs. If you have something not on that list, ask us and we’ll tell you honestly whether we’re the right fit.

Do you care for snakes?

We care for non-venomous snakes such as ball pythons and corn snakes, following your feeding and handling notes. We do not care for venomous snakes.

How often do my pets need a visit?

It depends on the species. Birds and small mammals generally need a visit every day, while fish and some reptiles may need less. We set the schedule with you at the meet-and-greet.

What if a heat lamp, heater or filter stops working?

We check them every visit. If something fails, we call you right away, fix what we safely can, such as a reset timer or a spare you’ve left out, and contact your vet if your pet needs it.

Do you need vaccination records for my pets?

Not for birds, reptiles, fish or most small pets. We ask for a current rabies certificate for dogs, cats and ferrets only.

Is the price different from a regular visit?

No. Visits for these pets are the same $30 as any 30-minute visit, with +$5 for each additional pet or habitat.
