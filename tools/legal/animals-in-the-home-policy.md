This policy is part of the Owner Service Agreement and the Terms of Service. It applies to every booking.

## 1. The rule in one paragraph

You must tell us about **every live animal** that will be in or on your home or property during any visit or stay, including animals you are not asking us to care for. Every animal beyond the first is charged at the published additional-animal rate for each visit or night. A sitter who is in your home cannot be asked to ignore an animal that is there, and automatic feeders, waterers, litter systems, cameras or "they take care of themselves" do not change the count.

## 2. What counts as an animal in the home

An animal counts if it lives in, is kept in, or will be in or on the home or property during a visit or stay. That includes:

- Dogs, cats, rabbits, guinea pigs, rodents, ferrets and other small mammals.
- Birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish.
- Animals that belong to a family member, roommate, tenant, houseguest or relative, and foster animals.
- Animals that are indoor-outdoor, shy, hidden, crated, caged or kept in a separate room, garage or outbuilding.
- Animals that are old, sleepy, or "no trouble."

A single tank or enclosure that holds fish or other small animals of the same kind counts as **one** animal. Separate tanks or enclosures count separately.

Animals that live on a neighboring property and are not on your property do not count.

## 3. How you are charged

- The base price of a booking covers the first animal in the home.
- Each additional animal in the home is billed at the published additional-animal rate (shown on our pricing page, currently $5 per animal for each visit or night) for every visit or night in the booking.
- Example: you book care for two dogs and there is also a cat in the home. That is three animals, so you pay the base price plus two additional-animal charges for each visit or night. The cat is charged even though you did not ask for the cat to be fed.
- The additional-animal charge applies to dog walks, drop-in visits, overnight stays and home check-ins, because the sitter is inside the home and responsible for noticing every animal there. It does not apply to meet-and-greets or to pet taxi rides.
- The booking form asks for the number of animals in the home and shows the total before you pay.

## 4. Why an animal in the home cannot be ignored

A sitter who enters a home is responsible for noticing hazards, including an animal that is injured, loose, out of water, trapped, or in distress. For that reason:

- The sitter will look in on every animal in the home at every visit or night and will report anything that looks wrong.
- You cannot instruct a sitter to skip, avoid, or not check on an animal in the home. You can tell us the level of care you want for each animal (for example "full care," "check water and look only," or "do not handle"). The additional-animal charge applies at every care level.
- Automatic feeders, automatic waterers, self-cleaning litter boxes, pet cameras and similar devices are welcome, but they do not remove an animal from the count and do not change the sitter's duty to check on it. Devices fail, and the sitter is the one who will find out.
- A sitter is not required to give medication, handle, feed, or clean up after an animal you did not book for care, unless you add that service in advance. A sitter may do so in an emergency to protect the animal.

## 5. Telling us about animals

- List every animal on your New Client Form and keep your pet list current in your client portal.
- At each booking you will confirm the number of animals that will be in the home. If it changes after you book (a new pet, a visiting dog, a foster), update the booking at least 24 hours before the visit.
- Share anything the sitter needs to be safe: bite history, escape habits, animals that must be kept apart, and animals that are afraid of strangers.

## 6. If we find an animal you did not tell us about

- The sitter will report it to Porchlight and to you right away, with a photo if it is safe and appropriate.
- You will be billed the additional-animal rate for that animal for **every visit and night in the booking** and in any earlier booking in the past 12 months where it was in the home.
- If the animal is a safety concern, the sitter may decline to continue, leave the home, or ask for the animal to be separated. The visit is still billed in full if the sitter had to stop because an animal was not disclosed.
- Repeated or intentional non-disclosure is a breach of your agreement. Porchlight may pause or end your account.

## 7. Animals we cannot take

We do not accept animals that are venomous or dangerous to handle, animals that are unlawful to keep in North Carolina or South Carolina, wildlife, livestock, or animals the sitter is not comfortable caring for. A sitter may decline any animal for safety. If you tell us in advance, we will tell you before booking whether we can accept the animal.
