Plenty of cats bolt under the bed the moment a visitor arrives. Owners often assume that means a sitter is not an option. In practice, a shy cat is usually better off staying home with a sitter than being taken to a boarding facility full of new smells and sounds.

## Why home still wins for shy cats

A shy cat's biggest stress is unfamiliar surroundings. At home, they keep their own hiding spots, their own litter box and their own routine. The only new thing is a person, and a patient person can become a familiar one.

## How a good sitter works with a shy cat

- **Slow introductions.** The sitter comes in quietly, speaks softly and lets the cat approach, rather than reaching in.
- **Sitting at floor level.** Sitting down, avoiding direct eye contact and offering a hand to sniff are all cat-friendly signals.
- **Finding the cat every visit.** Even if your cat stays hidden, the sitter confirms they are eating, drinking, using the litter box and moving normally.
- **Using food and play.** A wand toy or a favorite treat can bring many shy cats out over a few visits.
- **Reading body language.** Ears, tail and pupils tell a sitter when to back off.
- **Never forcing contact.** Pressure makes shy cats hide for longer.

## How to set your sitter up

1. **Book a meet-and-greet first.** Let your cat see the sitter while you are home. Our [meet-and-greet guide](/blog/pet-sitter-meet-and-greet) explains what to expect.
2. **List the hiding spots.** Under the bed, behind the couch, inside the closet. This saves time and worry.
3. **Share what works.** A certain treat, a favorite toy, a word your cat responds to.
4. **Explain the warning signs.** Flattened ears, a swishing tail or a low growl mean "give me space."
5. **Say how you want escapes handled.** Tell the sitter which doors and windows must stay shut, and keep the carrier easy to find.

## What counts as a problem

Hiding itself is normal for a shy cat. These are not:

- Not eating or drinking for a full day
- Not using the litter box, or straining in it
- Vomiting or diarrhea that continues
- Hiding much more than usual, or hissing at things that do not normally bother them

In these cases, a sitter should contact you and your vet. See [what a sitter checks on every visit](/blog/caring-for-cats-while-away).
