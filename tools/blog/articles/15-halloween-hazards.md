Many of the most common pet poisonings involve things already in your home. Halloween adds candy, decorations and visitors, which makes this a good time to review what is unsafe.

> **If you think your pet ate something dangerous**, call your vet, an emergency clinic, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) or the Pet Poison Helpline (855-764-7661) right away. Fees may apply. Do not wait for symptoms, and do not induce vomiting unless a professional tells you to.

## Foods

- **Chocolate**, especially dark and baking chocolate. Toxic to dogs and cats.
- **Xylitol**, a sweetener in sugar-free gum, candy, some peanut butters, baked goods and toothpaste. Even small amounts can be dangerous to dogs.
- **Grapes and raisins**, which can cause kidney failure in dogs.
- **Onions, garlic, chives and leeks**
- **Macadamia nuts**
- **Alcohol and raw yeast dough**
- **Caffeine**
- **Fatty scraps and cooked bones**, which can cause pancreatitis or obstructions

## Halloween specifics

- **Candy:** Keep trick-or-treat bowls and bags out of reach, including wrappers, which can cause intestinal blockages.
- **Glow sticks:** Not usually deadly but can cause drooling and distress.
- **Decorations:** Candles, fake cobwebs, small parts and cords are chewing or entanglement hazards.
- **Costumes:** Only if your pet tolerates them. Make sure they do not limit vision, hearing, breathing or movement.
- **Door traffic:** Frequent doorbell rings and open doors increase the risk of escape. Consider a gate or a quiet room.
- **Pumpkins:** Plain pumpkin is generally safe in small amounts, but decorated or moldy pumpkins and candles are not.

## Plants

- **Lilies** are extremely dangerous to cats. Even pollen or vase water can cause kidney failure.
- **Sago palm** is highly toxic to dogs and cats.
- **Azaleas, oleander, tulip and daffodil bulbs, and autumn crocus** are also hazardous.

The ASPCA maintains a searchable toxic and non-toxic plant list online. Check it before you bring a new plant home.

## Other common household risks

- Human medications such as ibuprofen, acetaminophen and antidepressants
- Rodent bait, slug bait and some insecticides
- Antifreeze, which can taste sweet to animals
- Cannabis products and edibles
- Essential oils and some cleaners

## Prevention tips

- Store food, medications and cleaning products in closed cabinets.
- Use pet-proof trash cans.
- Tell guests and children not to share food with pets.
- Provide your sitter with a list of hazards in your home, and the poison control numbers.

Being prepared makes a poisoning scare less frightening, and quick action greatly improves the outcome.
