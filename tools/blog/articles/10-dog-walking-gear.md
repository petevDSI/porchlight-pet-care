Walks are more than bathroom breaks. They give dogs exercise, mental stimulation and a chance to explore the world through scent. A well-planned walk keeps dogs healthier, calmer and better behaved at home.

## How much exercise does a dog need?

It depends on age, breed, size and health. As a rough guide:

- **Puppies** need frequent short outings and play, with care not to overdo exercise while their joints develop.
- **Adult dogs** often do well with 30 to 60 minutes of activity a day, and high-energy working breeds may need much more.
- **Seniors** usually benefit from shorter, gentler, more frequent walks.
- **Flat-faced breeds** tire and overheat quickly and need shorter outings.

Ask your veterinarian what is appropriate for your dog's body condition and any joint or heart conditions.

## Harness or collar?

- **Flat collar:** Good for ID tags. Fine for dogs who do not pull.
- **Back-clip harness:** Comfortable and secure for many dogs, and reduces pressure on the neck.
- **Front-clip harness:** Gives more steering control for dogs who pull.
- **Head halters:** Effective for some strong pullers, but need gradual introduction.
- **Retractable leashes:** Less control, and they can cause injuries. A standard 4 to 6 foot leash is safer for neighborhood walks.

Choose gear that fits snugly without chafing, and check it often for wear. For escape artists, a martingale collar or a double-attached harness adds security.

## Make walks calmer

- **Let your dog sniff.** Sniffing is how dogs read the news and burns mental energy.
- **Reward loose-leash walking.** Treat and praise when the leash is slack.
- **Stop when pulling starts.** Stand still, and move forward again when the leash loosens.
- **Keep greetings controlled.** Ask other owners before letting dogs say hello.
- **Pick up after your dog**, every time.

## Safety checklist

- Carry water on warm days and see our [hot weather walking guide](/blog/hot-weather-dog-walking)
- Watch for hazards such as broken glass, antifreeze puddles and dropped food
- Be aware of wildlife, loose dogs and traffic
- Keep ID tags on at all times, and confirm microchip registration

## Handing off to a dog walker

Share with your walker:

1. The usual route and any places to avoid
2. Your dog's triggers
3. Whether leash greetings are allowed
4. Gear location, treats and rules for taking treats from strangers
5. How you want to be updated, such as a text and photo after each walk

> **Remember:** A tired dog is a happier dog. A good walk before you leave for work or travel makes the rest of the day easier.

Regular, well-matched exercise keeps dogs fit and can prevent many behavior problems before they begin.
