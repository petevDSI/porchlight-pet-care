Can You Leave a Cat Alone for a Weekend? Risks and a Safer Plan

By Porchlight Pet & Home Care · November 12, 2026 · 2 min read

Cats are often described as low-maintenance, and a healthy adult cat will usually survive a weekend with food and water left out. Surviving is not the same as being safe and comfortable. Here is what can go wrong in 48 hours alone, and what to do instead.

What can go wrong

  • Water and food run out or go bad. Bowls get knocked over, and wet food spoils quickly. Automatic feeders can jam.
  • Litter boxes fill up. Cats can be picky about dirty boxes, and some will go elsewhere in the house.
  • A hidden health problem escalates. Cats hide illness well. Urinary blockage, which affects male cats most often, is a life-threatening emergency, and International Cat Care notes it can cause kidney failure within a few days if untreated. Someone who sees your cat every day is far more likely to catch it.
  • Accidents. A cat stuck behind a door, a knocked-over plant, a chewed cord or a loose window screen can become serious when no one is home.
  • Stress. Cats who are left alone with no company can become anxious, which in some cats shows up as hiding, overgrooming or urinating outside the box.

Who can reasonably stay alone

Every cat is different, so ask your vet if you are unsure. As a general rule:

  • Healthy adult cats on a short trip are the best candidates, ideally with a daily check-in.
  • Kittens, seniors and cats with medical needs should not be left unchecked. They need meals, medication or monitoring on a schedule.
  • Male cats with any history of urinary problems need someone watching the litter box.
  • Multi-cat homes can develop tension while you are gone, and a sitter helps spot it.

A safer weekend plan

  1. Arrange at least one visit a day. Even a short visit covers fresh water, a scooped box, a health check and a hello. See how often a sitter should visit.
  2. Use backups, not replacements. A fountain and an automatic feeder are helpful, but they do not notice a sick cat.
  3. Add a second litter box if you are gone more than a day.
  4. Cat-proof the house. Close off hazards, secure windows and put away cords and string. Lilies are extremely toxic to cats, as the ASPCA lists, so keep them out of the house.
  5. Leave clear instructions. Food amounts, vet contact, hiding spots and quirks. Our instruction sheet guide covers what to include.

What a visit looks like

At Porchlight, a cat visit is about 30 minutes: meals and water, a scooped box, play, a health check and a photo and note sent to you afterward. A weekend of visits costs about $90 for one cat. You can see how pricing adds up or go straight to cat sitting.

Bottom line: Many cats will be fine alone for a weekend, but "fine" depends on one thing going right for 48 hours. A daily visit takes that risk away.
