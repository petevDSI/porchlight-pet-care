Animals in the Home Policy

Version 2026-11-13.5 · applies to owners and sitters

This policy is part of the Owner Service Agreement and the Terms of Service. It applies to every booking.

1. The rule in one paragraph

You must tell us about every live animal that will be in or on your home or property during any visit or stay, including animals you are not asking us to care for. Every animal beyond the first is charged at the published additional-animal rate for each night of an overnight stay. On walks, drop-ins and home check-ins, an additional animal is charged only if it needs a service from the sitter. Fish and reptiles that need no care are never charged. A sitter who is in your home cannot be asked to ignore an animal that is there, and automatic feeders, waterers, litter systems, cameras or "they take care of themselves" do not change the count or the overnight charge.

2. What counts as an animal in the home

An animal counts if it lives in, is kept in, or will be in or on the home or property during a visit or stay. That includes:

  • Dogs, cats, rabbits, guinea pigs, rodents, ferrets and other small mammals.
  • Birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish.
  • Animals that belong to a family member, roommate, tenant, houseguest or relative, and foster animals.
  • Animals that are indoor-outdoor, shy, hidden, crated, caged or kept in a separate room, garage or outbuilding.
  • Animals that are old, sleepy, or "no trouble."

A single tank or enclosure that holds fish or other small animals of the same kind counts as one animal. Separate tanks or enclosures count separately. Fish and reptiles must be disclosed even though they are usually not charged (see section 3).

Animals that live on a neighboring property and are not on your property do not count.

3. How you are charged

  • The base price of a booking covers the first animal in the home that needs care from us.
  • Overnight stays: each additional animal in the home is billed at the published additional-animal rate (currently $5 per animal for each night). This applies whether or not you asked us to care for the animal, and automatic feeders, waterers and litter systems do not change it.
  • Walks, drop-ins and home check-ins: an additional animal is billed at the same rate for each visit only if it needs a service from the sitter on that visit. If the additional animals need nothing from the sitter, there is no charge for them on that visit. The booking form asks you to say which applies.
  • Fish and reptiles: a fish tank or reptile enclosure is never charged if it needs no care from the sitter (for example, it is on an automatic feeder and heater and nothing needs to be done). If it needs care, it is treated like any other additional animal. The booking form asks how many tanks or enclosures need no care.
  • What counts as a service: feeding, giving water, cleaning litter or a cage or tank, giving medication, letting an animal out or in, playing with or walking an animal, brushing or other grooming, adjusting lights, heat or misting, or anything else you ask the sitter to do for that animal. One-time action by the sitter to protect an animal in an emergency is not a service and is not charged.
  • Example 1: you book an overnight for two dogs and there is also a cat in the home. That is three animals, so you pay the base price plus two additional-animal charges for each night, even though you did not ask for the cat to be fed.
  • Example 2: you book a 30-minute drop-in for your dog. There is also a cat on an automatic feeder and a fish tank, and you tell us they need nothing on this visit. There is no additional-animal charge. If the sitter has to scoop the cat's litter, the cat is billed for that visit.
  • Meet-and-greets and pet taxi rides have no additional-animal charge.
  • The booking form asks for the number of animals in the home and shows the total before you pay.

4. Why an animal in the home cannot be ignored

A sitter who enters a home is responsible for noticing hazards, including an animal that is injured, loose, out of water, trapped, or in distress. For that reason:

  • The sitter will look in on every animal in the home at every visit or night and will report anything that looks wrong. A look is not a service and is not charged on a walk, drop-in or check-in.
  • You cannot instruct a sitter to skip, avoid, or not check on an animal in the home. You can tell us the level of care you want for each animal (for example "full care," "check water and look only," or "do not handle").
  • Automatic feeders, automatic waterers, self-cleaning litter boxes, pet cameras and similar devices are welcome. They do not remove an animal from the count for an overnight stay and do not change the sitter's duty to check on it. Devices fail, and the sitter is the one who will find out.
  • A sitter is not required to give medication, handle, feed, or clean up after an animal you did not book for care, unless you add that service in advance. A sitter may do so in an emergency to protect the animal.

5. Telling us about animals

  • List every animal on your New Client Form and keep your pet list current in your client portal.
  • At each booking you will confirm the number of animals that will be in the home. If it changes after you book (a new pet, a visiting dog, a foster), update the booking at least 24 hours before the visit.
  • Share anything the sitter needs to be safe: bite history, escape habits, animals that must be kept apart, and animals that are afraid of strangers.

6. If we find an animal you did not tell us about

  • The sitter will report it to Porchlight and to you right away, with a photo if it is safe and appropriate.
  • If an additional animal was present and not included in your booking, we may invoice the additional-animal rate shown when you booked for each visit or night that we can substantiate. A photo from Tuesday supports Tuesday's charge. Earlier dates need their own evidence, such as your confirmation, dated visit records or other reliable documentation. We look back no further than 12 months, and only to bookings where the animal was present.
  • The invoice shows the dates, the rate, the basis for the correction, and how to dispute it. At launch we send an itemized payment request. We will not charge a saved card for an adjustment unless you have given express authorization that covers these adjustments, including how we calculate them and when we charge.
  • If the animal is a safety concern, the sitter may decline to continue, leave the home, or ask for the animal to be separated. The visit is still billed in full if the sitter had to stop because an animal was not disclosed.
  • Repeated or intentional non-disclosure is a breach of your agreement. Porchlight may pause or end your account.

7. Animals we cannot take

We do not accept animals that are venomous or dangerous to handle, animals that are unlawful to keep in North Carolina or South Carolina, wildlife, livestock, or animals the sitter is not comfortable caring for. A sitter may decline any animal for safety. If you tell us in advance, we will tell you before booking whether we can accept the animal.

8. Questions

If you are not sure whether something counts, count it and ask us. Email support@porchlightpetcare.com.
