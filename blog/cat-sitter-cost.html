How Much Does a Cat Sitter Cost? What Changes the Price

By Porchlight Pet & Home Care · November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Cat sitter rates are set by each sitter or company, so there is no single national price you can count on. What you can do is understand what drives the total, so you can compare quotes fairly. Below are the factors that matter, followed by what we charge at Porchlight in the Belmont, NC area.

What changes the price

  • Visit length. A 30-minute visit covers food, water, litter, play and a health check for one or two cats. Shorter "quick check" visits cost less but leave little time for the rest.
  • Number of cats. Many sitters include one or two cats and add a small fee for each additional pet.
  • Visits per day. Kittens, seniors and cats on medication often do better with two visits a day, which doubles the visit count.
  • Holidays. Christmas, Thanksgiving and other peak dates usually carry a surcharge because sitters have the fewest people available.
  • Overnight stays. A sitter who sleeps at your home costs more than a daily visit, but suits cats who are anxious or do best with company.
  • Medication. Some sitters charge extra for injections such as insulin. Ask up front.
  • Travel distance. Sitters who charge a trip fee will cost more if you live outside their usual area.

What Porchlight charges

  • Cat visit: $30 for about 30 minutes, one or two cats
  • Each additional pet: +$5 per visit
  • Holidays: +$10 per visit
  • Overnight stay: $99, with the sitter staying the night

Current prices are always on the cat sitting page.

Three example trips

These use our listed prices and one cat, with one visit a day.

  1. A weekend (Friday to Sunday, 3 visits): 3 x $30 = $90
  2. A week (7 visits): 7 x $30 = $210
  3. A week for a cat on twice-daily medication (14 visits): 14 x $30 = $420

A second cat in the same home adds $5 per visit, so the weekend above becomes $105 for two cats.

Is it cheaper than boarding?

It depends on the cat count and trip length, but the comparison is not only about money. Boarding means a carrier ride, a strange place and exposure to other animals. Many owners find the difference worth it for a cat who dislikes change. We cover the tradeoffs in pet sitter vs. boarding.

How to compare quotes

Ask each sitter the same questions so the numbers line up:

  1. How long is a visit, and what does it include?
  2. Is there an extra charge for a second cat, medication or holidays?
  3. Do you send photos and notes after each visit?
  4. What happens if you are sick or cannot make a visit?
  5. Do you offer a free meet-and-greet before the first booking?
Tip: The cheapest quote is rarely the best value if the visit is too short to scoop the box, wash the bowls and actually spend time with your cat.

How often should someone visit?

Once a day suits most healthy adult cats on a short trip. Kittens, seniors and cats with health needs do better with two. Our guide to how often a sitter should visit walks through the options.
