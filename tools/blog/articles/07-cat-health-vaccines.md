Cats are masters at hiding illness. A quick pre-trip checkup is a good way to catch problems before you are away from home and your sitter is the first to notice something is wrong.

> **Important:** Your veterinarian will recommend a vaccine plan based on your cat's age, health and lifestyle. This article is general information only.

## Core vaccines

Feline vaccination guidelines treat these as core for all cats:

- **Rabies.** Required by law in North Carolina and South Carolina, and important even for indoor cats, since bats occasionally enter homes.
- **FVRCP.** A combination vaccine protecting against feline viral rhinotracheitis, calicivirus and panleukopenia.

## Non-core vaccines

- **Feline leukemia (FeLV).** Recommended for kittens and for cats who go outdoors or live with cats who might be infected. Your vet will advise on adult indoor cats.
- Other vaccines may be considered based on risk.

## What to do before you leave

1. **Schedule a wellness exam.** Ask your vet to check weight, teeth, kidneys and heart, especially for senior cats.
2. **Confirm parasite prevention.** Fleas, ticks and heartworm preventives matter even for indoor cats. See our [parasite prevention guide](/blog/flea-tick-heartworm-prevention).
3. **Update the microchip registration** and make sure the collar has an ID tag with a breakaway clasp.
4. **Trim nails and brush the coat** if your cat tolerates it, particularly for long-haired cats.
5. **Ask about stress.** If your cat becomes anxious when you leave, your vet can suggest strategies.

## What your sitter should know

- What food your cat eats and how much, plus whether wet food is refrigerated
- Where the litter box is, and how often it is changed
- What normal looks like: appetite, hiding spots, favorite play times
- Medications, dosage and how your cat accepts them

## Signs your sitter should call the vet

- Not eating for more than a day
- Straining in the litter box, crying while urinating, or no urine output (especially in male cats, which can be an emergency)
- Repeated vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy
- Labored breathing or open-mouth breathing
- Sudden hiding, aggression, or a change in behavior

> **A word on urinary blockages:** If a male cat is straining to urinate and producing little or nothing, treat it as an emergency and go to a vet immediately.

A thoughtful pre-trip health check helps your sitter focus on keeping your cat comfortable instead of puzzling over unfamiliar symptoms.
