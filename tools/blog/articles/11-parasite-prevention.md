In the Carolinas, fleas, ticks and mosquitoes can stay active long after summer ends. Veterinary parasite experts recommend year-round prevention for most dogs and cats, because the diseases these parasites carry can be serious and expensive to treat.

> **Important:** Prevention products differ by species, weight and health. Never use a dog product on a cat, and ask your veterinarian before switching products.

## Heartworm

Heartworm disease is spread by mosquitoes and can damage the heart and lungs. It affects dogs and cats, and prevention is far easier than treatment.

- Prevention is typically a monthly chewable, topical or a longer-acting injection from your vet.
- Dogs are generally tested annually, since missed doses can leave them unprotected.
- Cats can get heartworm too, even if they live indoors, and prevention is available.

## Fleas

Fleas cause itching and can transmit tapeworms and other diseases. A few fleas become an infestation quickly, in the pet and in the home.

- Use a veterinarian-recommended preventive, year-round.
- Wash bedding regularly and vacuum often.
- If you find fleas, treat all pets in the household and talk to your vet about treating the environment.

## Ticks

Ticks can carry Lyme disease, ehrlichiosis, anaplasmosis and other infections. They can be active any time temperatures are above freezing.

- Use a tick preventive recommended by your vet.
- Check your dog after walks in grass or woods: between toes, under the collar, in the ears, around the tail and in armpits.
- To remove a tick, use fine-tipped tweezers close to the skin and pull straight out with steady pressure, then clean the bite.

## Before a sitter stay

1. **Time the dose.** Make sure your pet's monthly dose will not be due while you are away, or leave clear instructions.
2. **Label everything.** Keep doses in original packaging with the date given.
3. **Brief your sitter.** Tell them what to look for after walks, such as ticks, and who to call if they find one.
4. **Mention any skin issues.** Pets with allergies or sensitive skin may react to certain products.

## Intestinal parasites

Roundworms, hookworms and whipworms are common, especially in puppies. Many monthly heartworm products also protect against some intestinal worms. Annual fecal testing is a good routine, and a sitter should pick up waste promptly to reduce reinfection.

## When to call the vet

- Persistent scratching, hair loss or skin irritation
- A tick bite followed by fever, limping, loss of appetite or lethargy
- Coughing, tiredness or reduced exercise tolerance, which can be signs of heartworm

Consistent prevention is one of the simplest, most effective ways to keep your pet healthy, and it makes a sitter's job safer and easier too.
